The single biggest factor in how accurate your BRIEF outputs are is the quality of the audio you give it. BRIEF processes what it hears — a clear recording leads to more accurate transcription, better speaker separation, and richer outputs. Getting the recording right costs you nothing and makes a bigger difference than any software setting.

<Tip>
  The most impactful improvement you can make is phone placement. A phone in the center of the table, face up, with no obstacles between it and the speakers, will outperform any external microphone placed poorly.
</Tip>

## Phone Placement

Where you put your phone determines what BRIEF hears. Follow these guidelines for every recording:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Place your phone centrally on the table">
    Position your phone in the middle of the table — or as close to the center of the group as possible. This gives every speaker roughly equal distance to the microphone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep it face up">
    The primary microphone on most phones is on the bottom edge or front face. Placing the phone face up keeps the microphone exposed and unobstructed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove obstacles">
    Don't slide papers, a laptop, or a bag over the phone. Even a thin sheet of paper between the phone and the room will noticeably reduce audio clarity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stay within 1–2 meters of the farthest speaker">
    BRIEF's speech recognition performs best when all speakers are within 1–2 meters of the phone. In a large room or long table, consider moving the phone closer to the end with more participants, or use a Bluetooth microphone placed centrally.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Don't cover the microphone">
    Avoid gripping or resting your hand over the phone during recording. Also check that your phone case doesn't obstruct the microphone opening — some thick cases partially block it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Reducing Background Noise

Background noise is the most common cause of transcription errors. BRIEF applies automatic noise reduction, but loud or constant noise will still affect accuracy.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Fans & Air Conditioning" icon="wind">
    Avoid placing your phone near fans, AC vents, or air purifiers. The constant hum is picked up strongly by close-field microphones and interferes with speech detection.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Windows & Doorways" icon="door-open">
    Close windows if street noise, traffic, or construction is audible. Doorways to busy corridors let in intermittent noise that disrupts speaker diarization.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Paper & Table Tapping" icon="hand">
    Ask participants to avoid shuffling papers or tapping on the table near the phone during the meeting. Contact noise is picked up directly through the table surface.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Parallel Conversations" icon="comments">
    If multiple groups are talking in the same space, move to a quieter room or closed office. BRIEF cannot reliably separate your conversation from another group speaking nearby.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  BRIEF displays a recording health indicator during active recording. A **green** indicator means audio is coming through clearly. **Yellow** means background noise has been detected. **Red** means the audio quality is likely to affect transcription — consider pausing and improving your setup before continuing.
</Note>

## Room Acoustics

The surfaces in your room affect how sound reaches the microphone.

Hard, reflective surfaces — glass walls, marble floors, tiled rooms — create reverb that makes speech harder to process. Soft furnishings absorb sound and reduce echo. If you regularly record in a reverberant room:

- Choose a room with carpets, curtains, or soft seating where possible.
- Move the phone closer to speakers to reduce the effect of reflected sound.
- Consider an external microphone with a directional pickup pattern (cardioid), which rejects sound from the sides and rear.

## Far-Field Recording

If your meeting room is large, or you regularly record with participants seated far from each other, a Bluetooth or wired external microphone placed centrally will significantly improve accuracy.

<Accordion title="Using an external microphone with BRIEF">
  BRIEF supports any microphone that connects to your phone — Bluetooth, Lightning, USB-C, or 3.5mm. When an external microphone is connected, BRIEF automatically uses it as the audio input.

  For large rooms, a small omnidirectional conference microphone placed in the center of the table is the most effective setup. These are available at a range of price points and make a substantial difference in rooms larger than four meters across.

  You do not need to configure anything in BRIEF — just connect the microphone before you start recording.
</Accordion>

## Android Battery Optimization

On certain Android devices, the operating system will terminate background apps — including BRIEF — to save battery. This can cause BRIEF to stop recording when you switch to another app or when your screen turns off.

The following manufacturers are known to have aggressive battery management that can interrupt BRIEF recordings: **Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Huawei, and Samsung**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Battery Optimization settings">
    Go to **Settings → Battery → Battery Optimization** on your device. The exact path varies by manufacturer and Android version.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find BRIEF and set it to unrestricted">
    Locate **BRIEF** in the app list. Set its optimization mode to **Don't optimize**, **Unrestricted**, or **No restrictions** — the label varies by device.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable Auto-launch for BRIEF">
    If your phone has an **Auto-launch** or **Background activity** setting for apps (common on Xiaomi MIUI and Oppo ColorOS), enable it for BRIEF. This allows BRIEF to maintain its background process during recording.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the fix">
    Start a short test recording, lock your screen for 30 seconds, then return to BRIEF. If the recording is still active, the setting is working correctly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Until you disable battery optimization for BRIEF, your phone may silently stop recording when the screen turns off. You will not receive a warning — you'll simply find a shorter recording than expected when you return to the app.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  Search **"disable battery optimization [your phone brand]"** for manufacturer-specific instructions. DontKillMyApp (dontkillmyapp.com) also has step-by-step guides for most Android manufacturers.
</Tip>

## During the Meeting

A few simple habits during the meeting itself will improve your recordings:

- **Keep the phone visible.** Don't cover it with papers or let it get buried under items on the table.
- **Ask participants to speak toward the phone** when possible — especially in larger rooms or when someone has a quieter voice.
- **Avoid moving the phone** during the recording. Handling noise is picked up strongly and can disrupt transcription of speech immediately before and after the movement.
- **Don't place the phone inside a bag or pocket** while recording — the microphone will be obstructed and audio will be significantly degraded.
