Standard study tools give you generic explanations from generalized sources. BRIEF study tools give you structured materials built from your specific lecture, in your lecturer's words, matched to exactly what your exam will test. Every flashcard, quiz question, and AI tutor answer traces back to a timestamp in your recording — so you can always verify where the content came from and hear it in context.

## Each Tool in Detail

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="1. Structured Summary">
    After processing a lecture recording, BRIEF generates a structured summary of the session — organized by topic, not just chronologically. Use this as your first review immediately after class while the material is fresh. The summary is in the same language mix as the lecture — Arabic sections stay in Arabic, English technical content stays in English — so the terminology matches exactly what your lecturer used.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="2. Key Concepts and Definitions">
    Every defined term, introduced formula, and stated principle is extracted and presented as a named concept with its definition. This becomes the vocabulary list for the course — built from what your specific lecturer said, not from a generalized glossary. Tap any concept to jump to the moment in the recording where it was introduced.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="3. Q&A Extraction">
    BRIEF identifies questions raised during the lecture — by the lecturer as rhetorical prompts, by students in the room, or as exam-prep cues — and pairs them with the answers given. This includes answers the lecturer gave in passing: a question in passing usually signals an exam question in waiting. The Q&A section surfaces these moments so you don't miss them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="4. Flashcards">
    One concept per card. The front of each card carries a term or a question; the back carries the definition or answer. Every flashcard is linked to the timestamp in the recording where the concept was introduced — if a card seems wrong or incomplete, tap it and hear the original explanation in context. Flashcards are designed for active recall: cover the back, read the front, test yourself before revealing the answer.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="5. Quiz">
    BRIEF generates questions that test understanding of the lecture material — not just recall of definitions, but application of concepts as the lecturer explained them. Every correct answer is cited from the lecture with a timestamp. If you disagree with an answer, you can verify it against the source recording. The quiz difficulty calibrates to the lecture content — a lecture that introduced basic concepts produces different questions than one that assumed prior knowledge.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="6. Learning Gap Detection">
    BRIEF analyzes the recording for signals that indicate you may have incomplete understanding of a concept: terms that appeared without full definition, concepts the lecturer returned to for re-explanation, and topics flagged explicitly as commonly misunderstood. These are surfaced as a prioritized review list with links to the relevant moments in the recording. Learning gaps are not failures — they are targeted study priorities.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="7. AI Tutor">
    Ask any question about the lecture in Arabic or English. The AI tutor answers only from what was actually said in the recording — it will not invent an explanation, draw from a textbook, or generate a plausible-sounding response that was not grounded in the lecture. Every answer includes a citation to the timestamp where the relevant content appears. If the recording doesn't contain an answer to your question, the tutor says so.

    <Tip>
      The AI tutor is grounded in your specific lecture — it knows what your lecturer said and how they explained it. Ask 'how did Dr. Ahmed explain recursion?' and it answers from the recording, not from a generic definition. This is the difference between a study tool that helps you prepare for your exam and one that gives you a different explanation than the one you'll be tested on.
    </Tip>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Course Memory and Semester-Long Study

Individual lectures are useful. A full semester's worth of recorded lectures, organized and searchable, is a different kind of resource entirely.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Label every lecture consistently">
    Use the same course name for every lecture in a course — 'ECON 201', for example. BRIEF groups all recordings under the course name and treats them as a unified archive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Search across the whole semester">
    In the Conversation Archive, search within a course for any topic — 'market segmentation', 'supply curve', 'externalities'. BRIEF returns results across every lecture you've recorded in that course, not just the most recent one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter Revision Mode before exams">
    Open the course in BRIEF and tap **Revision Mode**. BRIEF surfaces the most important concepts across all lectures in the course, unsolved questions flagged during the semester, and suggested flashcard sets for high-priority topics. Use this in the week before an exam to identify what deserves the most attention.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask the tutor cross-lecture questions">
    In Revision Mode, you can ask the AI tutor questions that span multiple lectures — 'how did the definition of supply change between lecture 3 and lecture 7?' or 'what did we cover about pricing in total across the semester?'. The tutor answers from the full course archive.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tips for Effective Study Use

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Recording Strategy">
    Record in Mode A (offline, on-device) during the lecture — this requires no campus Wi-Fi and captures audio entirely on your device. After the lecture, switch to Mode C when you're on a reliable Wi-Fi connection. Cloud processing provides significantly better accuracy for academic terminology, mixed-language content, and flashcard and quiz generation quality.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Highlights During the Lecture">
    Use the highlight button during the lecture to mark moments that feel important — a definition being stated for the first time, an example the lecturer returns to, a concept flagged as likely to appear on the exam. Highlights anchor the flashcard generation and tell BRIEF which moments were most significant. A lecture with good highlights produces better-targeted study materials than one without.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Spaced Repetition">
    Review flashcards the same day as the lecture — within a few hours if possible, while the material is fresh. BRIEF will suggest a spaced-repetition schedule: the first review is the most important, and subsequent reviews are spaced further apart as the material moves from short-term to long-term memory. Follow the schedule for each course across the semester and arrive at exam week with material that is already consolidated, not material you're seeing for the second time.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## How the Provenance System Works

Every piece of content BRIEF generates from a lecture is linked to the exact moment in the recording where it came from. This applies to flashcards, quiz answers, key concepts, Q&A pairs, and AI tutor responses.

<Note>
  This is the fundamental difference between BRIEF and AI tools that generate study content from general knowledge. A generative model can produce a plausible definition of any concept — but your exam is based on what your lecturer said, not on what the model considers the canonical explanation. BRIEF only produces content from your recording.
</Note>

When you tap any piece of generated content — a flashcard, a concept, a quiz answer — BRIEF jumps to the timestamp in the recording where it originated. You hear the explanation in the lecturer's own words, in context, at the moment it was delivered. If the content seems wrong, you can verify it against the source. If the source confirms the content is accurate, you know you can rely on it.

## Privacy and Responsible Use

<Warning>
  BRIEF does not support sharing lecture audio recordings with others. Sharing the recording file — by forwarding it, uploading it, or providing access to others — may violate your university's academic integrity policy and applicable law. Sharing your own study materials (summaries, flashcards, notes) derived from a lecture is your personal decision; sharing the recording itself is not covered by that same latitude.
</Warning>

Keep your recordings labeled clearly by course and date. Do not share recording access with classmates. The study materials BRIEF generates are personal to your recording and your learning — they are not a substitute for attending and engaging with the lecture yourself.
