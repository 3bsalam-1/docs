Sales conversations are the highest-stakes conversations in any business — and they're documented the worst. Objections, competitor mentions, pricing signals, and next steps are recorded in a CRM hours after the meeting, reconstructed from memory that has already started to fade and soften. BRIEF captures the client meeting as it happens, extracts what matters, and gets a professional follow-up to the client before you've left the building.

## What Gets Lost Between the Meeting and the CRM

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Softened Objections" icon="filter">
    The objection was specific and pointed in the room. By the time you log it, it's 'client had some concerns about pricing'. The exact language — the language that tells you what to address — is gone.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Verbal Commitments" icon="handshake">
    The client said they'd include you in the Q3 budget conversation. You said you'd send the technical spec by Friday. Neither commitment made it into the CRM accurately.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Competitor Intelligence" icon="chart-bar">
    The client mentioned a competitor by name and said something specific. You remember the name but not the context. The context was the useful part.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Delayed Follow-Up" icon="clock">
    The follow-up email arrives the next morning — when the conversation is 18 hours old and the client has already moved on mentally. The window for reinforcing what was agreed has closed.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## What BRIEF Extracts from Sales Conversations

BRIEF's Professional Conversation output captures the signals that matter most in client-facing work. The specific categories appear when the conversation contains them — BRIEF surfaces what was actually said, not a template filled with guesses.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Needs and Pain Points">
    Every problem the client named, every friction point they described, and every outcome they said they were looking for — extracted and attributed to the moment in the conversation where they said it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Objections Raised">
    Objections are captured verbatim, not summarized. The exact language the client used when they pushed back is preserved. Tap any objection to hear the full context in the recording.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Commitments — Both Parties">
    What you committed to and what the client committed to are both extracted and linked to their timestamps. 'You agreed to include X' becomes 'you said at 23:14: [exact quote]'.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Competitor Mentions">
    Any competitor named during the conversation is flagged, along with the context in which they were mentioned. Useful for account strategy and for CRM intelligence.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Pricing Signals">
    Budget ranges mentioned, pricing concerns raised, and any signal about willingness to invest are captured when they appear in the conversation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Next Steps Agreed">
    The specific next step agreed at the end of the meeting — who does what, by when — is extracted as an action item with owner and deadline.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  BRIEF's Professional Conversation output captures requirements and commitments especially well in client-facing conversations. The specific sales categories — needs, objections, competitor mentions — appear automatically when the conversation contains them. You don't configure extraction categories manually.
</Note>

## Core Workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set up the recording before the client arrives">
    Open BRIEF and enter the client's name and the meeting type in the setup screen. This labels the recording in your archive and makes it searchable by client name later. Select Mode C for fastest processing, or Mode A if you're in a location without reliable connectivity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable the announcement prompt">
    In recording settings, turn on the **Announcement Prompt**. BRIEF displays a large Arabic/English 'recording in progress' notice on screen. Turn the screen toward the client at the start of the meeting — this sets expectations professionally and handles disclosure without an awkward verbal announcement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run the meeting normally">
    Conduct the client meeting as you always would. Speak naturally in Arabic, English, or the mix that the conversation calls for. BRIEF handles code-switched Arabic/English and regional dialects — Egyptian and Gulf — without any special handling on your part.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop and review — in under 2 minutes">
    Tap **Stop** when the meeting ends. In Mode C, the full structured output is ready in under two minutes. Review the extracted commitments and next steps while the client is still present if appropriate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the محضر before leaving">
    Tap **Share via WhatsApp** and send the formatted محضر to the client's number or group. Do this before you leave the room or the parking lot. The client receives a professional Arabic record of everything discussed and agreed while the meeting is still fresh.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Archive for CRM entry">
    The full structured output — needs, objections, commitments, competitor mentions, next steps — is saved in your BRIEF archive. Use it as the source for your CRM entry. Every field is accurate because it came from the actual conversation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The محضر as a Follow-Up Tool

In business culture across Egypt and the Gulf, a prompt and professional written record after a client meeting signals seriousness and respect. The BRIEF محضر is formatted for Arabic professional communication — the structure, the register, and the level of detail are calibrated for this context.

Many sales professionals in the region use the BRIEF محضر as their standard post-meeting follow-up. It:

- Shows the client exactly what was discussed and what was agreed
- Reinforces your professionalism and attention to detail
- Creates a shared written record that both parties refer back to
- Eliminates the 'I thought we agreed on something different' conversation before it happens

<Tip>
  Sending the محضر to the client before you've left the parking lot is the single most impactful habit BRIEF users develop. It demonstrates attention, creates a shared record of what was agreed, and puts your name in the client's WhatsApp at the moment their memory of the meeting is sharpest.
</Tip>

## Searching Past Client Conversations

Your BRIEF archive is a complete record of every client engagement from the first prospecting call to the close.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="By Client Name">
    Search the client's name in the Conversation Archive. Every recorded meeting with that client appears in order — first contact, discovery call, demo, negotiation, and close. See how the conversation evolved and what was committed at each stage.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="By Keyword">
    Search 'budget', 'Q3', 'competitor name', or any keyword from the engagement. BRIEF retrieves every moment across all recordings where that term appeared — with a tap-to-jump link to the audio.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="By Commitment">
    Search for a specific commitment — 'technical spec', 'pilot program', 'pricing proposal' — to find every conversation where it was mentioned. Useful before a follow-up call when you want to recall exactly what was said and when.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Privacy When Recording with Clients

Recording a client conversation requires transparency. BRIEF is designed to make disclosure natural rather than awkward.

<Warning>
  Always disclose that you are recording before the conversation begins. Use the Announcement Prompt — it provides a clear Arabic/English 'recording in progress' screen that handles disclosure visually, professionally, and without interrupting the meeting setup.
</Warning>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Announcement Prompt">
    Enable this in BRIEF's recording settings. It displays a prominent bilingual recording notice on your screen. Turn the screen toward the client as you place your phone on the table. Most clients in the region respond positively — it signals structure and professionalism.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Mode A for Sensitive Deals">
    For conversations involving sensitive commercial terms, pricing negotiations, or confidential client information, use Mode A. All processing happens on-device. Audio never leaves your phone.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Mode C for Speed">
    For standard discovery calls and follow-up meetings where speed matters, Mode C delivers the structured output in under two minutes — ready before the client has reached the elevator.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Tips for Sales and Client-Facing Roles

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Label every recording with the client name and stage">
    'Nour Telecom / Discovery Call' and 'Nour Telecom / Proposal Review' are searchable in an instant six months later. Unlabeled recordings are effectively lost.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use the محضر as a scope confirmation">
    After a requirements or discovery conversation, forward the Requirements section from the BRIEF output in a follow-up message: 'Based on our conversation, here is my understanding of your requirements — please confirm.' This converts a verbal discovery session into a written scope agreement.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Review objections before the next call">
    Before any follow-up call, search the previous meeting in your archive and review the Objections section. The exact language the client used when they pushed back is your roadmap for what to address in the next conversation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use competitor mentions for account strategy">
    Any competitor flagged in the BRIEF output goes into your account strategy notes. The context in which the competitor was mentioned — favorably, skeptically, as a reference point — is more useful than the mention alone, and it's preserved in the transcript.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Don't wait to enter the CRM — use the BRIEF output directly">
    The BRIEF structured output contains everything you need for a CRM entry: needs, objections, commitments, next steps, and dates. Copy it directly. You're not reconstructing from memory — you're transcribing from a record.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
