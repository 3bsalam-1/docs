Managing a team means making and receiving dozens of commitments every week across 1:1s, standups, project reviews, and ad-hoc conversations. Most of those commitments exist only in memory — and memory degrades, misfires, and rewrites itself in favor of the rememberer. BRIEF captures the raw conversation, extracts what was said and by whom, and gives you a searchable record of every promise made in both directions.

## What Gets Lost Without a Record

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Forgotten Promises" icon="clock-rotate-left">
    A commitment made to a direct report is a trust debt. When you forget it and they don't, you've damaged the relationship — not just missed a task.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Blurred 1:1s" icon="comments">
    Weekly 1:1s blur together. What did you agree to follow up on last week? What did Ahmed say was blocking him two weeks ago? Without a record, the next 1:1 starts from scratch.

## What BRIEF Extracts from Management Conversations

BRIEF's Professional Conversation output is particularly well-suited to management conversations because these conversations are dense with exactly what BRIEF is trained to extract.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Action Items with Owner and Deadline">
    Every action item is extracted with an assigned owner and, where stated, a deadline. After a 1:1, you see a clean list: what you committed to, what your report committed to, and when each item is due. Share this list via WhatsApp before the conversation ends.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Decisions with Context">
    Each decision is captured with the sentence that produced it, linked to the exact moment in the recording. When someone asks 'why did we decide to drop feature X?', you tap the decision and hear the conversation that led to it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Commitments — in Both Directions">
    Commitments made by you and commitments made by your report are both surfaced. Search the Conversation Archive by a person's name to see every commitment made in every conversation with them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Follow-Up Items">
    Questions that weren't resolved, topics that need a second conversation, and items explicitly flagged as 'follow up on this' are extracted to a dedicated section — so nothing falls through between meetings.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Risks">
    Anything surfaced as uncertain, at risk, or dependent on external factors is flagged under Risks. In a project meeting, this gives you a standing risk log built automatically from the conversation.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Core Workflows

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="1:1 Meetings">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Start recording at the top of the 1:1">
        Open BRIEF, enter your team member's name and the date, and tap **Record**. Tell your team member BRIEF is recording — transparency here is important (see the Privacy section below).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Run the 1:1 normally">
        Cover your agenda as usual. Discuss blockers, progress, career topics, feedback — whatever the conversation calls for. BRIEF is actively recording — the screen shows the recording state throughout the conversation.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Review the output together">
        Tap **Stop** when done. Review the action items and commitments together while you're both still present. Correct anything that was misattributed.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Share the action list via WhatsApp">
        Send the extracted action items to your report via WhatsApp. They leave the meeting with a written record of what was agreed. You have the same record in your BRIEF archive.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Open the archive before the next 1:1">
        Before the following week's 1:1, open the previous session in BRIEF. Review open action items, unresolved follow-ups, and commitments from both sides. Start the meeting with full context.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Standups">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Record the standup as a group conversation">
        Tap **Record** and run the standup as normal. BRIEF handles short-form conversations well and supports multiple speakers.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Review per-person output">
        After the standup, BRIEF surfaces per-person status, blockers, and commitments for the day — organized by speaker where speaker labels can be inferred.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Share blockers and actions">
        Forward the extracted blockers and today's commitments to the team WhatsApp group. Everyone sees the same summary.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Project Meetings">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Record the full meeting">
        Start BRIEF at the beginning of the project meeting. Enter the project name so this meeting is grouped correctly in the archive.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Use the decision log">
        After the meeting, every decision extracted is linked to the exact moment in the recording where it was made. This becomes your living decision log for the project.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Distribute action items with owners">
        Share the extracted action item list — with owners and deadlines — to the relevant WhatsApp group or via PDF export. Action items from a project meeting should never live only in someone's memory.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## The Decision Log Use Case

Every project accumulates decisions that are later questioned. 'Why did we choose that vendor?' 'Who decided to cut that feature?' 'When did we agree to push the deadline?' These questions are expensive to answer after the fact — they require reconstructing a conversation from the memories of people who each remember it differently.

With BRIEF, every decision extracted from a project meeting includes:

- The decision itself, stated in plain language
- The sentence from the conversation that produced it
- A tap-to-jump link to the exact moment in the recording

When someone asks 'why did we decide that?', the answer is one tap away — not a reconstruction, but the original conversation.

<Note>
  The decision log is most powerful when you record all project meetings consistently. A single missed meeting creates a gap in the record. Make recording the default, not the exception.
</Note>

## Tracking Commitments to Your Team

Search the Conversation Archive by a team member's name to see every commitment made in every recorded conversation with them. Use this before performance reviews, before difficult conversations, and before any meeting where the question 'didn't you say you would...' might arise.

<Tip>
  At the start of each 1:1, tell your team member BRIEF is recording and show them the recording state screen. Most people appreciate having a written record of what was agreed — it protects them too, not just you.
</Tip>

## Privacy for Management Conversations

1:1 conversations are among the most sensitive in any organization. BRIEF gives you control over how they are processed and stored.

<Warning>
  Use Mode A (on-device processing) for 1:1 conversations that involve performance, compensation, health, or other sensitive personal topics. In Mode A, audio never leaves the device — there is no cloud upload at any point.
</Warning>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Mode A — On-Device">
    All processing happens locally on your phone. Audio never leaves the device. Ideal for sensitive 1:1s, HR conversations, or any meeting where confidentiality is paramount. Processing is immediate.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Mode B — Hybrid">
    Anonymised processing split between device and cloud. Suitable for most project meetings and standups.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Mode C — Cloud">
    Full cloud processing. Fastest turnaround, highest accuracy. Use for large group meetings or when you need the output in under two minutes.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

You can also exclude specific conversations from the searchable archive — they remain accessible directly but do not appear in archive searches. Use this for conversations that should stay entirely private.

## Tips for Managers

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Label every recording with the person's name">
    Entering your team member's name in the recording setup makes their full conversation history instantly searchable. Before a quarterly review, search their name and see every 1:1, every commitment, every decision across the whole period.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use follow-up items as your 1:1 agenda">
    The Follow-Ups section from last week's 1:1 is this week's opening agenda. Open the previous recording before the meeting and start by reviewing open items. This creates continuity across meetings that would otherwise reset every week.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Don't edit the محضر before sharing">
    Many managers share the BRIEF output with their team without editing it. This signals that the record is objective — it's what was actually said, not a summary filtered through the manager's perspective.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Record your own commitments deliberately">
    When you make a commitment to a report — 'I'll get you the budget approval by Thursday' — say it clearly during the meeting. BRIEF will extract it. This makes your follow-through visible and builds trust over time.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
