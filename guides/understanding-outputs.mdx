After every recording, BRIEF produces a structured set of outputs tailored to the type of conversation you had. Rather than giving you a wall of text to read through, BRIEF surfaces the facts that matter — decisions made, actions assigned, risks flagged — each linked back to the exact moment in the audio where it was said. This guide explains what each output section means and how to get the most from it.

---

## The Two Conversation Experiences

BRIEF produces different output sets depending on whether your conversation is classified as Professional or Generic. You can see — and change — the current classification using the conversation type chip at the top of every output screen.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Professional Conversation">
    Professional outputs are designed for business meetings: client calls, project reviews, vendor negotiations, team standups, and any conversation where accountability matters.

    <CardGroup cols={2}>
      <Card title="Summary" icon="file-lines">
        A concise paragraph summarising what the conversation was about, who was involved, and what was resolved.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Key Points" icon="list-check">
        The most important things said — condensed into scannable bullets.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Decisions" icon="gavel">
        What was decided, who decided it, and when. Each decision links to its timestamp in the audio.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Action Items" icon="circle-check">
        Every commitment to do something, with the assigned owner and deadline. Tap any action item to hear the moment it was agreed.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Risks" icon="triangle-exclamation">

      </Card>

## The Conversation Type Chip

Every output screen shows a small labelled chip near the top — for example **Professional** or **Generic**. This is BRIEF's classification of your conversation.

If BRIEF got it wrong — say it classified a vendor negotiation as Generic — you can fix it in seconds:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tap the type chip">
    The chip is tappable. Tap it to open the type selector.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the correct type">
    Select Professional or Generic (and the relevant sub-type, if applicable).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap Regenerate">
    BRIEF re-runs the extraction layer against your stored transcript. This is fast — there's no re-transcription. Only the structured outputs are rebuilt.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Regeneration does not reprocess your audio. It re-runs the extraction and summarisation step against the transcript that's already been saved. You won't lose any edits you made to the transcript itself.
</Note>

## Speaker Names

BRIEF labels speakers as **Speaker 1**, **Speaker 2**, and so on. For many conversations, BRIEF's language model infers probable names from context — mentions in the conversation, calendar data, or your contact history — and suggests them automatically.

To confirm or correct a speaker name:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tap a speaker label">
    Tap any instance of "Speaker 1" (or the suggested name) anywhere in the output.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type the correct name">
    Enter the name as you'd like it to appear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Tap confirm. BRIEF updates every mention of that speaker across the entire output — summary, decisions, action items, transcript — retroactively.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Correct speaker names before sharing. A محضر that reads "Ahmed agreed to send the proposal by Thursday" is far more useful — and more professional — than one that reads "Speaker 2 agreed to send the proposal by Thursday."
</Tip>

## Provenance: Tapping Back to the Source

Every extracted fact in a Professional output — every decision, action item, requirement, commitment, and risk — is linked to the moment in the recording where it was said.

Tap any item to jump to that timestamp in the audio. Use this to:

- Verify that BRIEF captured the exact wording of a commitment
- Hear the tone or context around a decision
- Resolve a disagreement about what was actually agreed

This link between output and audio is called **provenance**. It's what separates BRIEF's outputs from notes — every fact is traceable.

## Editing Outputs

All output fields are editable. BRIEF's extraction is a starting point, not a final document.

<Accordion title="Adding an item">
  Tap the **+** icon at the bottom of any section to add a new bullet, action item, or decision manually.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Editing an existing item">
  Tap any extracted item to enter edit mode. Change the text, reassign the owner, update the deadline, or reword the summary.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Removing an item">
  Swipe left on any item, or enter edit mode and tap delete. Removed items don't affect the transcript or audio.
</Accordion>

<Note>
  Manual edits are preserved if you regenerate the output — BRIEF merges your edits with the new extraction rather than overwriting them. Review the merged result after regeneration to make sure everything looks right.
</Note>

## Bilingual Output

BRIEF generates outputs in two languages by default:

- **Arabic محضر** — a formal Arabic summary of the conversation, structured as a professional record (محضر اجتماع). Suitable for sharing with Arabic-speaking clients and colleagues as-is.
- **English action items** — extracted actions, owners, and deadlines in English, useful for tools and workflows that expect English input.

The balance between Arabic and English in the output adapts to your conversation. A fully Arabic meeting produces a fully Arabic محضر. A code-switched meeting produces a محضر that reflects that mix naturally.

You can adjust the default language preference in **Settings → Output Language**.

<Tip>
  The محضر is designed to be sent to clients or colleagues as-is, without editing. The formatting, register, and structure match the conventions of a formal Arabic meeting record. Many BRIEF users send the محضر directly from the output screen before they've left the meeting room — that's the intended workflow, not a shortcut.
</Tip>
